Start Here – Welcome to IB Film

This site is your central hub for IB Film Studies at Sixth Form. It brings together theory, analysis, production skills, assessment guidance, and curated resources to support you throughout the course.

  • Learn the core language of film: cinematography, editing, sound, mise-en-scène, performance.
  • Practise textual analysis with guided questions and sample responses.
  • Develop practical production skills for your portfolio and collaborative film project.
  • Understand each IB assessment and how to meet the criteria.
  • Explore case studies of key films and movements to deepen your understanding.

Use the navigation above to jump to a section, or follow this suggested path:

  • Begin with Film Theory to build your vocabulary.
  • Move into Textual Analysis to practise close reading of scenes.
  • Explore Production Skills as you plan and create your own work.
  • Regularly check IB Assessments so you know what you’re working towards.

How to use this site

Think of this site as a living textbook and toolkit. It won’t replace watching films or making them, but it will help you organise your thinking and prepare for assessments.

  • Use it during lessons as a reference and for tasks set by your teacher.
  • Return to it when revising or planning coursework.
  • Follow the prompts and questions – don’t just read, respond.
  • Bookmark key pages that feel most relevant to your current project.

Textual Analysis Skills

Learn to read film closely and express your ideas clearly.

How to Analyse a Scene

A practical approach to close reading.

  • Watch the scene multiple times – once for story, then for detail.
  • Identify key moments: turning points, emotional peaks, visual motifs.
  • Note cinematography, editing, sound, and mise-en-scène choices.
  • Ask: how do these choices shape meaning and audience response?
  • Connect the scene to the film’s wider themes and context.

Annotating Clips

Organising your observations.

  • Use timestamps to anchor your notes.
  • Separate description (what happens) from analysis (why it matters).
  • Highlight recurring patterns and contrasts.
  • Link technical choices to character and theme.
  • Turn your annotations into structured paragraphs.

Writing Textual Analysis

From notes to a coherent essay.

  • Begin with a clear focus: what aspect of the scene are you analysing?
  • Use topic sentences to guide each paragraph.
  • Integrate technical vocabulary naturally – don’t just list terms.
  • Support points with specific examples from the scene.
  • Conclude by linking back to the film’s overall meaning.

Sample Analysis (Teacher Models)

Examples of strong responses.

  • Annotated exemplar of a scene analysis.
  • Highlighting effective use of terminology.
  • Demonstrating clear structure and argument.
  • Showing how to balance description and interpretation.
  • Identifying where the response meets IB criteria.

Production Skills

Practical tools for planning, shooting, and editing your own films.

Camera Basics

Using the camera with intention.

  • Understanding exposure: aperture, shutter speed, ISO.
  • Framing and composition – rule of thirds and beyond.
  • Stability: tripod, handheld, gimbal.
  • Focus control and depth of field.
  • Planning shot lists to support your story.

Lighting

Shaping mood and visibility.

  • Three-point lighting basics.
  • Natural vs artificial light.
  • Hard vs soft light and diffusion.
  • Colour temperature and gels.
  • Using shadows and contrast creatively.

Sound Recording

Capturing clean, usable audio.

  • Microphone types: lavalier, shotgun, handheld.
  • Monitoring levels and avoiding clipping.
  • Reducing background noise and echo.
  • Recording room tone and ambient sound.
  • Syncing sound with picture in post-production.

Editing Fundamentals

Building your film in the timeline.

  • Organising footage and naming files.
  • Rough cut vs fine cut.
  • Basic transitions and continuity.
  • Integrating sound, music, and effects.
  • Exporting in appropriate formats and resolutions.

Storyboarding & Script

Planning before you shoot.

  • Visualising key moments with simple drawings.
  • Shot-by-shot planning for complex sequences.
  • Script formatting basics (sluglines, dialogue, action).
  • Rewriting and refining for clarity.
  • Aligning script and storyboard with production schedule.

Directing & Collaboration

Working with people, not just equipment.

  • Communicating vision to cast and crew.
  • Giving constructive direction to actors.
  • Managing time on set.
  • Respecting roles and responsibilities.
  • Reflecting on collaboration in your IB work.

Production Workflow

From idea to finished film.

  • Development: idea, research, treatment.
  • Pre-production: script, storyboard, schedule, casting.
  • Production: shooting, sound, directing.
  • Post-production: editing, sound design, colour.
  • Reflection: evaluating process and outcome.

Real locations, non-professional actors, social focus.

Experimentation, self-reflexivity, and playfulness.

  • Influence on modern independent cinema.
